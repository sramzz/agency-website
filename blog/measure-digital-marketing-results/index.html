To judge whether your digital marketing is working, follow the useful enquiries and work it helps bring in, then compare those outcomes with the costs. Visits, clicks and reported conversions help explain part of that picture. They do not establish the business result on their own.

Say you run a maintenance business in Adelaide. Your campaign report shows more conversions, but your team needs to know which requests fit the services you offer and which became worthwhile jobs. This is a hypothetical example, not a Ranking Rebels client case.

Start with the outcome your business needs, keep reliable records and show where the evidence stops. An attributed sale is different from proof that marketing created an additional customer. A small set of defined measures gives you a more useful starting point than a dashboard containing every available metric.

Start with the result the business needs

“Grow the website” leaves too much open. Does the business need more suitable quote requests for a particular service, more bookings within its capacity, or better returns from an existing acquisition budget?

Write the objective in business terms, with the service, audience and outcome in scope. Then identify what you can observe along the way.

An educational article or awareness campaign can have a legitimate intermediate purpose. Judge that purpose with appropriate evidence rather than requiring every visit to produce an immediate enquiry. Equally, do not present engagement with an article as demonstrated revenue without the link being established.

Define an enquiry, a qualified lead and a customer

Start with the records your team uses to handle work. A click on a phone number is an interaction. A received call or message establishes contact. If visits are arriving but useful requests are scarce, our guide to website visits but few enquiries helps you investigate the page and contact route. Qualification checks whether the requested service, location and other agreed criteria fit. A new customer needs its own definition, such as a first accepted job under your sales process.

Keep those stages distinct. Someone might submit a form and then call about the same project. Counting both as independent leads would inflate acquisition performance unless your definition deliberately treats them as separate opportunities.

Track won opportunities separately from new customers. An existing customer can accept another job, and one customer can have more than one opportunity. Identify first-time customers against the business's customer records, rather than treating every accepted quote as a new customer.

Record qualification and sales outcome separately. A suitable enquiry can still be lost because the customer chose another provider or the business lacked capacity. An enquiry awaiting assessment is not automatically unsuitable; an open quote is not automatically a lost sale.

For a practical starting record, capture an enquiry reference, received date, available source information, assessment status and reason, responsible person, next action and eventual outcome. Keep personal details in authorised business systems rather than copying them into public reports or general analytics fields.

A conversion label does not verify the business outcome

Google Analytics describes important behavioural actions as key events, and also supports conversions used to measure advertising performance. The meaning depends on the action and report being used. Google's explanation of key events and conversions sets out the distinction.

Marking an event as important does not prove that a valid enquiry arrived or a customer bought. Check the configured action against the receiving and sales records. If an authorised test is appropriate, label its records so that testing does not become ordinary lead reporting or sales follow-up.

Give each tool a specific job

Different reports answer different questions. They are useful together, but they do not automatically describe the same people or use interchangeable units.

For the paid-search side, our guide to how Google Ads works for service businesses follows a search through the ad, enquiry and recorded outcome.

Search Console clicks are not Analytics sessions. A person can return to the website, a session can contain multiple events, and website measurement may not capture every interaction. Google's Search Console and Analytics comparison explains why totals can differ.

Within Analytics, the Traffic acquisition report focuses on how sessions are acquired; user acquisition addresses a different question about initial acquisition. Specify the report and metric before comparing sources. Do not rename a session count “people”, “leads” or “customers”.

Also be careful with Direct traffic. Google defines it as traffic without a clear referral source. It can include missing source information, so an increase is not automatically evidence that more people remembered your brand and typed its address.

Work out why the reports disagree

If a campaign report says 40 conversions and the sales system records 25 enquiries, investigate what each number means before deciding which is wrong.

Check five things:

  1. Action and unit. Is the report counting events, sessions with an event, received enquiries or customers? Does it include repeat actions or test records?
  2. Scope and filters. Are the service, channel, pages, geography and included conversion actions comparable?
  3. Dates. Is the figure assigned to the ad interaction date or the outcome date? Are time zones, observation cut-offs and processing status aligned?
  4. Attribution and counting. Which model, eligible interactions, window and counting rules apply?
  5. Coverage and implementation. Are tags, source information, contact delivery and record updates working as intended? What cannot be associated reliably?

Google documents differences between Analytics-derived conversions and Google Ads reporting, including timing and counting. Do not assume every column uses the same date basis. For Australian businesses, also check the day represented by the report: Search Console's daily reporting uses Pacific Time, except for its 24-hour view, rather than automatically matching your local business day. Its discrepancy guidance explains this and other differences.

Expected differences do not prove that an implementation is healthy. A sudden break still deserves investigation. If you cannot explain the remaining gap, retain it as an explicit limitation rather than forcing the numbers to match.

Source labels help, but they do not reconstruct every journey

For appropriate external campaign links, consistent campaign parameters can help identify the source, medium and campaign in Analytics. Google's current URL builder guidance explains these parameters and their case-sensitive values. Coordinate manual tagging with any platform integrations or auto-tagging already in use, rather than changing links blindly.

Ask how the customer heard about the business when that fits the enquiry process. Treat the answer as another piece of evidence, not a perfect record of every influence. Some enquiries will still have unknown or incomplete source information.

Keep names, email addresses and message contents out of URLs, campaign parameters and ordinary event fields. Google provides guidance on avoiding personal information in Analytics. In Australia, the OAIC's tracking pixel guidance also explains the need to understand collection, disclosure and applicable obligations. A larger dataset is not automatically a better measurement design.

Follow enquiries long enough to see the outcome

Suppose a customer accepts a quote in November after enquiring in October. Dividing November's advertising spend by that sale without considering its origin can distort acquisition performance.

For acquisition questions, follow a defined group of enquiries or advertising interactions through its outcomes. This is a cohort: records grouped by a stated origin and period. Show when the group was observed, how long it has been followed and how many outcomes remain unresolved.

Google calls the time between an ad click and a conversion conversion lag. Your sales process may add further time from enquiry to quote, acceptance, delivery and payment. A recent campaign can therefore look different after its outcomes develop.

Compare groups with a reasonably comparable opportunity to progress. Also account for service mix, seasonality, promotions, pricing, capacity and changes to tracking. A before-and-after comparison is useful evidence, but it does not isolate which change caused the result.

With small numbers, one extra job can create a large percentage movement. Show the counts beside rates. If you cannot reliably associate enquiries with website sessions, report their counts separately instead of inventing a complete funnel or a conversion rate with mismatched units.

Define what each cost and rate includes

A cost per lead only helps when “lead” and the included cost are defined. An inexpensive received enquiry may be poor value if it requests work you do not offer. A more expensive suitable enquiry may still be uneconomic if delivery costs or the sales process make the job unattractive.

For enquiries in the same defined group:

  • Qualification rate: qualified enquiries divided by unique received enquiries, with assessment coverage disclosed.
  • Qualified-enquiry win rate: qualified enquiries that resulted in an accepted job divided by qualified enquiries in the same group, with unresolved outcomes disclosed. Count each enquiry once as won, even if it produces several jobs.
  • Included marketing cost per new customer: stated marketing costs divided by new customers associated with the group.

If the denominator is zero, the rate or cost is unavailable; show the underlying counts and spend. Do not turn it into a zero acquisition cost. A session-based conversion rate is a different calculation: it needs a session-based numerator and denominator, rather than a count of repeated events divided by sessions.

Separate new-customer acquisition from repeat-customer work when interpreting costs. If a campaign serves both, make the scope and cost allocation explicit rather than treating every job as acquisition.

Worked example: a 5× ROAS is not five dollars of profit

The following example is entirely hypothetical, not a benchmark, forecast or Ranking Rebels case study. All amounts are AUD, consistently excluding GST.

For the hypothetical Adelaide business, one campaign is linked to 30 separate enquiries: 12 suitable and 18 unsuitable. Three suitable enquiries become completed jobs for three different first-time customers; the other nine are lost. All enquiries have been assessed and no sales outcomes remain open.

ROAS counts revenue against media spend only: AUD 7,500 ÷ AUD 1,500 = 5×. It leaves out the AUD 600 of campaign and page work and the AUD 3,600 delivery costs.

The AUD 1,800 is what remains after delivery and included marketing costs, before other overheads and costs. It is not net business profit. The AUD 700 includes only the stated marketing costs, rather than complete company-wide customer acquisition cost.

These attributed results do not prove that advertising caused additional customers or provide a complete ROI calculation.

Google's ROI explanation considers costs as well as revenue. Also check what “value” means in the platform: a configured conversion value for an enquiry is an estimate, not automatically realised revenue. Do not add that estimate to the later job's revenue as though they were independent sales.

What attribution can and cannot tell you

Attribution assigns credit for an outcome under a model and its settings. A person may encounter more than one channel, so adding attributed conversions from separate platform reports does not establish a count of unique customers.

The different question is what happened because of the marketing compared with what would have happened without it. That is incremental impact. An attributed total alone does not answer it.

Models can be sophisticated: Google's data-driven attribution documentation describes counterfactual methods and training using randomised trials. That does not turn an ordinary attributed total into a campaign-specific lift study.

Google's Conversion Lift guidance describes treatment/control comparisons and notes that the tool is not available to every account. Appropriately designed experiments can help when the business question, data and resources justify them. Small businesses can still improve operational records and make informed decisions while acknowledging that causal impact remains uncertain.

Finish the review with a decision

A useful report should change what you investigate, maintain or do next. Tie the action to the point where the evidence becomes weak.

Choose a review rhythm around the decision and the sales cycle. Check delivery and measurement when launching or changing a journey; review enquiry handling often enough to act on it; evaluate customer economics after outcomes have had a suitable opportunity to develop. Those are different tasks, rather than a universal waiting period for every channel.

End with an observation, a possible explanation, a focused action, an owner and a next review. This makes measurement useful even when it cannot provide certainty. The benefit is a clearer basis for spending, follow-up and improvement, with the limitations visible alongside the results.
