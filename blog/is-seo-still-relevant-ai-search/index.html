Yes. SEO still helps people find your business and decide whether to contact you. ChatGPT and AI search give them other ways to ask questions and compare suppliers. A useful website gives them somewhere to check what you offer and take the next step.

Google says the foundations of SEO still apply to its AI search features. Keeping your pages accessible and useful remains part of that work. Google's guidance on generative search.

To see what this means for a service business, follow someone looking for an office cleaner in Melbourne.

A Melbourne example: choosing an office cleaner

Imagine a commercial cleaning company serving a defined part of Melbourne. This is a hypothetical example, not a client result.

A prospective customer might search for “commercial office cleaning Melbourne”. They might also ask an AI assistant what to check before hiring a company for after-hours cleaning. Both searches concern the same service, but they need different answers.

An AI answer could help them prepare a shortlist or think through the questions to ask. When they assess a particular company, they may still want to know whether it covers their address, what cleaning is included and how an after-hours visit would work.

That is useful work for the company's website. A service page can explain its actual coverage and scope, show an example of the work and describe how to request a quote.

SEO helps search engines understand those pages and helps people find them and decide whether to visit. The work still has a purpose when search results include an AI answer. Google's SEO Starter Guide.

Think about your own service pages in the same way. Would someone who has never spoken to you understand whether you can help them?

What SEO can still do for your business

For the cleaning company, a useful outcome would be a request from an office it can actually service. That gives the website work a clearer purpose than increasing traffic from anywhere, for any reason.

Help the right people find the right service

Explain the services you provide and the areas you cover. Someone looking for after-hours office cleaning should be able to check whether that work fits your offer.

Prioritise services you can deliver well. Then review whether enquiries match those services and locations. A large visit count tells you little about that fit on its own.

Help customers understand the offer

Service scope, exclusions, availability and the quote process help someone assess a supplier. If prospects keep asking the same question, use it to improve the page.

For our Melbourne example, that might mean clarifying what a site assessment involves or how access is arranged for after-hours work. Review the questions and requests received after the change to see whether the explanation helped.

Give people evidence they can check

Original photographs, a documented case or a worked example can support a claim about your service. The cleaning company could show an example of a cleaning scope, provided it reflects its actual operation.

Google encourages original contribution and accurate authorship information. Its guidance also says E-E-A-T isn't itself a specific ranking factor. A biography won't make an unsupported claim reliable. Google's helpful-content guidance.

Choose evidence that answers a customer's question. Review it against the objections your team hears, without assuming that adding a case study will increase conversions.

Keep useful answers available

A clear service explanation can keep helping people after publication. Your team can also share it when the same questions come up during a quote or booking.

It needs upkeep. Check changes to scope, prices and availability, and whether customers or your team still use the resource. That maintenance belongs in the cost of SEO.

Make the next step easier

Once someone thinks you might be suitable, give them an appropriate way to enquire. For the cleaning company, a quote form could ask for location, premises type, approximate size and preferred schedule.

The team could then record whether the request fits its service and whether it leads to a site visit or accepted quote. If suitable enquiries arrive but few progress, examine response times, capacity and the offer alongside acquisition.

What happens when an AI answer comes first?

Some people may get enough information from an AI answer without visiting a website. Businesses have a reason to watch how that affects their own pages.

Pew Research Center analysed browsing data from 900 US adults covering 68,879 unique Google searches in March 2025. People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits with an AI summary, compared with 15% without one. A source link within the summary received a click in 1% of visits with a summary.

The study was observational, the queries differed, and researchers reconstructed search results in April after recording the browsing. It describes that US sample. It doesn't predict Australian traffic or prove that AI caused the same loss for every website. Pew's original research and method.

The Melbourne customer may get general hiring advice from an AI answer. The cleaning company's page has a more specific role: explaining its service and helping someone assess a job. Review whether your important pages contribute useful enquiries as well as visits.

Google and ChatGPT are different routes

An answer about your business is worth checking for accuracy as well as presence. Google Search and ChatGPT Search have different systems, controls and reporting.

What your web team should check for Google

Google says a page must be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet to qualify as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode. There's no special AI schema requirement, and eligibility doesn't guarantee selection. Google's AI-feature documentation.

Your web team should also check the Search generative AI control in Search Console. Google's help page describes inclusion as the default, with settings that can be inherited from a parent property. Training preferences are separate from this control. Search generative AI control.

Google describes retrieving relevant pages and issuing related searches, called query fan-out, when building an answer. A ranking for the original search and a linked citation in the answer are different things to observe. This describes Google's approach, rather than every assistant's system. Google's explanation of retrieval and query fan-out.

What your web team should check for ChatGPT Search

OpenAI separates its search crawler, OAI-SearchBot, from GPTBot, which crawls content that may be used to train foundation models. Their controls are independent. A website can allow search access while disallowing GPTBot. Access alone doesn't guarantee a citation. OpenAI's crawler documentation.

ChatGPT may search automatically or when requested. Responses that use search may include citations, but results and citations can be incomplete, outdated or incorrect. Open any linked sources when checking an answer about your company. OpenAI's ChatGPT search help.

For the cleaning company, the job is to make accurate service information available and inspect how it appears in each system. It shouldn't assume that a position in Google determines what ChatGPT will say.

SEO, GEO and AEO in plain English

You may see these labels in agency proposals. They describe overlapping work. The definitions below are a practical way to discuss it, rather than a specification shared by every platform.

For the Melbourne company, service scope and coverage could matter in each setting. The platform checks would still differ. Advice about Google Search doesn't automatically describe ChatGPT Search, Gemini Apps or Microsoft Copilot.

You don't need to become fluent in every acronym to assess a proposal. Ask which pages will change, which customers they should help and how the agency will review the result.

Agree on review points around the work and the evidence you can collect. Completing a page update, appearing in a result and winning a job are separate outcomes.

Make one useful change and review it

The cleaning company could start with its office-cleaning page and the questions customers ask before requesting a quote. Your business can take the same approach with one priority service and a realistic service area.

Read the page as a new customer would. Ask your team which details they regularly have to explain, then address the most consequential gap. Record what changed and when.

Apply the same factual standards if you use AI to help write. Google's spam policies address large-scale production intended to manipulate rankings without helping users, however the pages were created. Google's spam policies.

If you're exploring AI presence, choose a small question set about your actual services, locations and customer decisions. Record the platform, date, mode, language and location context, and repeat observations under consistent conditions. Keep a brand mention separate from a recommendation or a linked source.

Aleyda Solis's prompt-library framework offers a professional method for choosing questions that reflect your audience. Your observations remain a sample, not an official ranking or a complete measure of Australian demand. Solis's prompt-library framework.

For the Melbourne company, a useful review would connect the revised page with the requests received and what happened to those requests. Keeping the stages separate helps identify where a problem remains.

People may encounter your business without clicking, return directly or use several channels before contacting you. Record what you can connect to an enquiry and where information is missing. Consider seasonality, advertising, platform changes and competitors before attributing a change to a page edit.

Two questions to ask an agency

Why are you recommending llms.txt?

Google says Search ignores llms.txt, so the file shouldn't be sold as a Google visibility requirement. Another system may use it. Ask which system the recommendation concerns and what the work is meant to achieve. Google's guidance on llms.txt.

What exactly are you promising to deliver?

Ask the agency to explain the work, platforms and reporting. If it promises a particular recommendation from an assistant, ask for the conditions and evidence behind that claim before relying on it.
