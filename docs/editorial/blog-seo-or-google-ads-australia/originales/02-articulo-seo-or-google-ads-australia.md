# SEO or Google Ads? How to Prioritise a Limited Marketing Budget

Prioritise a focused Google Search ads test when you have a clear offer, relevant search demand, a working enquiry process and enough budget to evaluate the response. Prioritise specific SEO work when there is a useful organic opportunity you can improve and support over an appropriate period. If the offer, contact route or evidence is unreliable, repair that gap before paying to attract more visits.

There is no universal budget split or month when one channel becomes the better investment. With limited money, the useful question is: **which next piece of work is worth funding, what will it cost and what evidence would justify continuing?**

This guide focuses on Australian service businesses and Google Search advertising. Other Google Ads campaign types have different mechanics. The framework and numerical example are educational; they are not a forecast, Australian benchmark or Ranking Rebels client result.

## Compare the work you would fund, not just the channel names

SEO aims to improve useful visibility in unpaid search results. Search ads buy opportunities to appear in paid placements under the campaign's rules and auction conditions. Google states that [advertising does not buy organic inclusion or rankings](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo).

Both can support relevant discovery and enquiries, but neither completes the sale for you.

| Decision | SEO | Google Search ads |
|---|---|---|
| What would you fund? | A defined improvement to access, content, structure or relevant evidence | A defined campaign, its destination, measurement and ongoing decisions |
| What costs matter? | Research, implementation, content, specialist help, maintenance and internal effort | Media, preparation, management, page work, measurement and internal effort |
| What can you control? | Your work and website; search systems select results | Campaign inputs and spending controls; eligibility and auctions still apply |
| What benefit might justify it? | More useful discovery and information that remains valuable while accurate and competitive | Relevant paid exposure, control over campaign scope and evidence about paid response |
| What should you avoid assuming? | Free work, guaranteed positions or permanent traffic | Immediate customers, fixed click costs or guaranteed profitable demand |

Compare actual proposals. “Improve the service page and resolve its indexing problem” is more assessable than “do SEO”. “Test one service in the area we can fulfil” is more assessable than “run Google Ads”.

## Define the need before allocating money

Specify the service, area, customer and outcome you want to support. Is the immediate need to fill available work, investigate demand for an offer or improve an important page that already receives relevant attention?

Then record the constraints: available funds, staff time, delivery capacity and how long you can evaluate the work. A business needing a result soon still needs a viable offer and economics; urgency does not make advertising profitable.

[Business.gov.au's marketing-plan guidance](https://business.gov.au/planning/business-plans/develop-your-marketing-plan) connects audience, goals, activities, estimated costs and evaluation. Use that sequence before deciding which channel deserves the next allocation.

Do not judge the decision only by a historical average cost per lead. An established organic page and a new paid test may have very different histories, costs and audiences. Identify the additional work and expenditure being considered now, while keeping a separate view of the channel's total costs.

## Check four things before committing

### 1. Is there relevant demand you can investigate?

Examine the language customers use, the services they request and the places you can serve. Separate searches for the business's name from searches by people looking for possible providers. Also distinguish service enquiries from information, employment or do-it-yourself searches.

Use existing Search Console, campaign and enquiry records where available. They reveal parts of the picture, not the whole market.

[Keyword Planner forecasts](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3022575?hl=en) can inform paid-search planning using selected locations, budgets and other assumptions. Its Competition metric concerns advertisers, not organic ranking difficulty. Forecasts are estimates, and search volume does not establish how many suitable enquiries will result.

No tool figures for a particular Australian industry or city were collected for this guide. Ask for assumptions relevant to your service rather than adopting a national CPC range from an unrelated market.

### 2. Can the page and team handle the response?

Check whether a suitable visitor can understand the service, recognise its scope and contact the right person. Confirm that a valid enquiry reaches the receiving system and has an owner.

A broken form, misleading offer or unanswered contact route affects the value of both channels. Repair the specific problem; this does not mean every business needs a new website before marketing.

Capacity matters too. More requests for work you cannot deliver may create cost without useful growth. Make the service address, availability and qualification criteria part of the plan.

### 3. Can the work support its included costs?

Revenue alone is not enough. Estimate what remains from an appropriate job after the delivery costs included in your calculation, then examine how marketing costs fit. State the cost basis and what remains excluded.

Do not justify acquisition using assumed lifetime value unless the business has evidence for retention, repeat work and the costs of serving it. A high quote value is not automatically a large amount available for marketing.

### 4. Can you observe a meaningful outcome?

Distinguish a contact click, unique received enquiry, qualified enquiry, accepted job and first-time customer. One person can make several contacts or buy more than once.

Begin with reliable business records and a defined source where known. Missing attribution stays unknown. You do not need to implement every advertising integration before making the records useful.

## When Google Ads may deserve the first allocation

Consider a bounded Search test when the business can describe a relevant offer, serve the likely demand and fund a test whose possible loss it can tolerate. The question might be whether a particular service attracts suitable enquiries at a cost worth investigating further.

Paid settings allow changes to parts of the targeting, message and investment. They do not create unlimited demand or precise control over every searcher.

Ads need [review for policy compliance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722120?hl=en) and still face [auction conditions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6366577?hl=en). An eligible campaign may not receive the expected delivery, and impressions or clicks do not guarantee enquiries. Do not plan essential income on “switch it on and leads arrive”.

A paid test can reveal something about the response to that paid offer and destination. It does not establish the whole market's demand, prove future organic rankings or validate an SEO programme automatically. Low delivery, poor measurement or a small sample can leave the test inconclusive.

The benefit is a controllable scope for learning and reaching relevant searches, provided the assumptions and limits are explicit.

## When SEO may deserve the first allocation

Consider SEO when you can identify a relevant organic opportunity and a specific improvement worth making: an important page is inaccessible, the service is poorly explained or useful evidence is missing from a page people need.

Start with the likely value and feasibility of that task, including whether your team can supply accurate information and implement changes. Avoid purchasing a quota of articles simply because SEO is described as a long-term investment.

SEO can improve a useful website resource without buying each organic click. The work still consumes money or time. Its continuing value depends on the page staying relevant, accessible and competitive; finishing a contract does not lock in future traffic.

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains that changes take time to be reflected and may not produce a noticeable effect. A technical correction and a competitive content programme need different milestones. Neither “SEO always takes six months” nor “waiting guarantees a lower lead cost” is a reliable basis for a budget.

Define what will be delivered, what useful change it is intended to support and when the available evidence can reasonably be reviewed. Keep implementation progress separate from search and customer outcomes.

## When to repair first, combine the channels or hold the decision open

The decision does not always have to select one channel immediately.

| Situation | Sensible next allocation to assess | Condition before extending it |
|---|---|---|
| Contact delivery or qualification records are unreliable | Repair the affected journey or measurement | Verify the intended record and ownership |
| A ready service needs a defined paid-response test | A narrow Search campaign with a complete allowance | Evaluate relevant enquiries and economics, not clicks alone |
| A useful organic page has a documented gap | The specific SEO/content/technical task | Verify implementation and review appropriate outcomes |
| Both have distinct, fundable roles | A sequence or combination with explicit responsibilities | Preserve enough scope and evidence for each; allocate shared costs once |
| Demand, economics or resources are too uncertain | Targeted investigation or a smaller preparatory task | Establish what would make further spending worthwhile |

A shared service page or measurement repair can help both channels. Do not count its full cost twice when comparing proposals. Equally, do not call internal time free merely because no agency invoice arrives.

Running both may make sense, but it is not a reason to divide every budget 50/50. If a split leaves neither piece of work able to answer its question, reduce or sequence the scope. There is no universal dollar threshold for that decision.

## A planning example: what would the test need to produce?

The following model is **entirely hypothetical**. All amounts are AUD, consistently excluding GST. It models first-time customers only, with one first job per customer. It supplies no observed conversion rate or probability of success.

Suppose the business is considering:

- A total test allowance of **AUD 2,400** for a defined period and acquisition group.
- **AUD 800** of included campaign-specific setup, management, page and measurement work.
- **AUD 1,600** left for media.
- An assumed average CPC of **AUD 8**.
- **AUD 500 per first job** remaining after the stated variable delivery costs, before marketing and other excluded costs. For this illustration, those delivery costs include the labour, materials and subcontracting attributable to that job.

If the media allocation were spent at that assumed CPC, the arithmetic gives 200 clicks. These are clicks, not unique people or enquiries, and they do not determine how many customers buy.

Test different customer-count assumptions without presenting them as forecasts:

| Hypothetical first-time customers, one job each | Contribution before marketing at AUD 500 per job | Balance after AUD 2,400 of included marketing costs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2 | AUD 1,000 | −AUD 1,400 |
| 4 | AUD 2,000 | −AUD 400 |
| 6 | AUD 3,000 | AUD 600 |

With these unchanged assumptions, five first jobs would cover only the included marketing cost: 2,400 ÷ 500 = 4.8, rounded up to five jobs. That is not a net-profit target, cash-flow forecast or proof of additional sales caused by advertising.

The model helps challenge the proposal: is the required result plausible enough to investigate, and can the business afford the unfavourable outcome? It does not answer those questions for you. A positive balance still precedes other costs and uncertainty about attribution.

Actual campaigns can also attract existing customers. Keep their work and value in the records; revisit scope and cost allocation rather than treating every accepted job as new-customer acquisition. Do not simply remove their value while leaving a mixed campaign's costs unexplained.

For SEO, prepare an equally explicit scope and cost record, but do not invent a smooth monthly lead curve to make it comparable. List implementation milestones, evidence of the opportunity, ongoing commitments and outcomes to observe. Uncertain future traffic should remain uncertain.

## Set review and stop conditions before spending

Record the question, service, geography, included costs, owner, permitted campaign scope and what would trigger investigation, a pause or further investment.

An internal allowance is not automatically a platform billing setting. Google documents [different campaign budget types](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486938?hl=en); verify the type and current charging rules applicable to the actual campaign. Keep media controls separate from the whole marketing allowance.

Check receipt and quality early enough to act on problems. Assess customer outcomes after the relevant group has had time to progress. Google calls the click-to-conversion delay [conversion lag](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9347141?hl=en); your enquiry, quoting and delivery process can add further time.

Keep unresolved outcomes visible and distinguish them from confirmed losses. A small test may end with “insufficient evidence”, not a winning channel. Do not extend spending indefinitely because a channel is labelled long term, or declare failure only because a recent enquiry has not yet had time to become a job.

## Compare combined business outcomes without double counting

Separate branded searches from discovery where the evidence allows. A person searching your business name may already know you through referrals, advertising or other activity. Neither a branded ad click nor a branded organic visit proves a newly created customer relationship.

Someone can use both channels. Reconcile unique business outcomes before adding totals from separate reports. Google's [paid-and-organic report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3097241?hl=en) can help examine search presence under its scope; it does not isolate what would have happened without an ad.

Attribution assigns credit under a model. Incrementality asks what happened because of the marketing compared with what would otherwise have happened. Google's [Conversion Lift guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/12003020?hl=en) describes treatment/control measurement and notes availability limits. An ordinary report is not a substitute for that causal comparison.

Do not assume paid and organic always add together, or that one always replaces the other. Use the best evidence available, disclose what remains uncertain and compare the next useful allocation rather than declaring a permanent winner.
