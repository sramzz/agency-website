# Facebook and Instagram campaign planning brief

An original educational resource prepared for Ranking Rebels on 21 October 2026 and revised on 22 October 2026. Examples and figures are fictional. They are not Australian benchmarks, campaign results or an experiment conducted by Ranking Rebels.

Use this brief to agree what a campaign should achieve and how the business will handle the response. It does not configure an advertising account or authorise spending, publishing, uploading customer data or contacting anyone.

Choose platform settings that serve the business outcome. A platform-reported action is not automatically a qualified enquiry, accepted job or new customer. Check current settings and availability rather than copying a screenshot or a default from an older guide.

Identify gaps that need resolving before a paid launch: a contact route that fails to deliver, an unsupported central claim or no owner for responses. Separate those from uncertainties that a planned test can legitimately investigate, such as the usefulness of two genuine creative concepts.

## 2. Turn a service into a creative hypothesis

Illustration: a fictional residential landscaping business serving selected Perth suburbs. Before using any claim, a real business would need to confirm the service, coverage, evidence, permissions and quote conditions.

| Concept | Question it addresses | Evidence to prepare | Suitable next step to confirm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explain the project process | What happens before work can begin? | Actual stages and the team's real responsibilities | Request information about a project |
| Explain a completed project | Can the provider handle a relevant type of work? | Genuine work, scope, context and permission to use material | Discuss a comparable project, without promising identical results |
| Answer a recurring scope question | Does the service fit this need and area? | Accurate inclusions, exclusions and coverage | Make an enquiry with the information needed to assess fit |

A hypothesis could be: “Explaining the scope and quote process may help suitable prospects provide a more relevant enquiry.” That is a proposal to evaluate, not an observed effect. Do not manufacture a testimonial, portfolio image or before-and-after result to make the concept convincing.

For each version, record the concept, asset, message, destination and intended outcome. Check readability, captions where appropriate, framing and contact information in the actual placements. Do not assume that changing a colour or cropping a video creates a meaningfully different message.

## 3. Verify the response journey

| Stage | What to confirm | Evidence / owner |
|---|---|---|
| Ad promise | Service, area, conditions and claims are accurate | |
| Destination or form | The same offer is explained and the next action is understandable | |
| Contact received | A retrievable message or record reaches the intended system | |
| Assessment | Agreed fit criteria are applied and pending cases stay visible | |
| Follow-up | A responsible person has an appropriate next action | |
| Business outcome | Won/lost opportunities and distinct new customers are recorded separately | |

Deduplicate repeat contact about the same opportunity. Keep qualification separate from sales outcome, and new customers separate from won jobs. A returning customer can buy again. If a controlled test is authorised, identify its records so they do not inflate lead totals or trigger normal sales follow-up.

## 4. Read cost per enquiry alongside quality

This illustration compares two fictional, non-overlapping groups of enquiries. Both have the same stated media spend and have been fully assessed using the same qualification criteria. There are no pending assessments. All figures are AUD, consistently excluding GST.

**This is not an A/B test.** The figures do not establish what caused a difference, which contact route is better or which campaign would be more profitable. No actual advertising was run.

| Measure | Illustration X | Illustration Y |
|---|---:|---:|
| Media spend | 1,200 | 1,200 |
| Unique received enquiries | 60 | 20 |
| Qualified enquiries within that group | 6 | 8 |
| Disqualified enquiries | 54 | 12 |
| Media cost per received enquiry | 1,200 ÷ 60 = 20 | 1,200 ÷ 20 = 60 |
| Qualification rate | 6 ÷ 60 = 10% | 8 ÷ 20 = 40% |
| Media cost per qualified enquiry | 1,200 ÷ 6 = 200 | 1,200 ÷ 8 = 150 |

The lower cost per received enquiry in X does not produce the lower cost per qualified enquiry in this arithmetic example. That is the only comparison established. Sales outcomes, revenue, delivery costs, creative production, management and follow-up costs are not provided. There is no evidence of profit, complete customer acquisition cost or incremental sales.

For a real review, record the group definition, observation cut-off, assessment coverage, attribution approach and costs included. If assessments are incomplete, label the quality comparison provisional and show the pending count. If a denominator is zero, report the measure as unavailable with counts and spend.

## 5. Plan learning without declaring an automatic winner

Write the question, metric and comparison before judging the result. Ordinary ad delivery can distribute exposure differently across versions; performance differences alone do not isolate a causal creative effect. If a controlled comparison is required, use an appropriate experiment design and verify the available tools and settings.

Record what changed and any other differences in offer, audience, destination, spend, timing or handling. Set a review around the information needed and the sales cycle. A fixed number of days, a few leads or the lowest reported cost is not a universal success rule.

| Observation | Next investigation | Owner / review |
|---|---|---|
| Many contact actions, few received enquiries | Check definitions and delivery | |
| Enquiries request work outside scope | Check message, audience assumptions and qualification | |
| Suitable enquiries are not followed up | Check ownership and capacity | |
| One creative appears better in ordinary delivery | Check evidence, allocation and whether a controlled test is needed | |

The brief is editorial synthesis, not a Meta template or a completed account audit. Use current primary documentation when implementing product settings, and keep private customer information out of public copies of this resource.
