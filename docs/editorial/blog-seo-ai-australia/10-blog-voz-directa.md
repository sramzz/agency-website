# Is SEO Still Relevant in the Age of ChatGPT and AI Search?

Yes. SEO still helps people find your business and decide whether to contact you. ChatGPT and AI search give them other ways to ask questions and compare their options. Your website still has a job: explain what you do, show why someone should consider you and make the next step easy.

Google says the foundations of SEO still apply to its AI search features. Keeping your website useful and accessible remains part of being found as search changes. [Google's guidance on generative search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

For a service business, start with the customer. What do they need to know before calling, booking or asking for a quote?

## Why your website still matters

Imagine you're choosing a company to clean your office. An AI answer might help you work out what to ask or suggest suppliers to consider. You may still want to check whether a company covers your area, offers after-hours cleaning and explains what its quote includes.

Those are questions your website can answer about your particular business. A clear service page gives someone a place to check the details and make contact when they're ready.

SEO helps search engines understand those pages and helps people find them and decide whether to visit. That remains useful when the results include an AI answer. [Google's SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

Read your service pages as someone who has never spoken to you. Would they understand what you offer, where you work and how to get started?

## What SEO, GEO and AEO actually mean

You may see all three terms in agency proposals. They describe overlapping work, although different platforms have their own systems and requirements.

| Term | What it means here | A useful question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| SEO: search engine optimisation | Helping relevant pages be found and understood in search | Can people looking for our services find useful information? |
| GEO: generative engine optimisation | Working on how a business appears in AI-generated answers | Is our business mentioned, linked to or described accurately? |
| AEO: answer engine optimisation | Making answers to customers' questions clear and useful | Does the page help someone resolve their question? |

You don't need to become fluent in every acronym to assess the work. Ask which pages will change, which customers they should help and how the agency will review the result.

Keep the platforms separate in that discussion. Advice about Google Search doesn't automatically describe ChatGPT Search, Gemini Apps or Microsoft Copilot.

## What good SEO helps customers do

Start with a service you actually provide and information a prospective customer needs. That creates several opportunities to improve your website.

### Find the right service in the right area

A service page can explain the work you do and where you do it. That helps someone check whether contacting you makes sense.

Prioritise services you can deliver well. When enquiries arrive, check whether they match your work and coverage. Traffic from locations you don't serve may contribute little to the business.

### Understand what they're buying

Service scope, exclusions, availability and the quote process help someone assess your offer. Leaving those details out may leave your team answering the same questions repeatedly.

Use those conversations to improve the page. If prospects keep asking whether you offer after-hours work, make the answer easier to find. Then review whether enquiries become more suitable or better informed.

### Check the evidence behind a claim

Original photographs, a documented case or an example of your work gives people something to assess. Choose evidence that answers a buyer's question and reflects work you can substantiate.

Google encourages original contribution and accurate authorship information. It also says E-E-A-T isn't itself a specific ranking factor. Adding a biography doesn't make an unsupported claim reliable. [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

Review that evidence against questions customers ask and objections your team records. That can show what's missing without assuming that adding a case study will increase conversions.

### Find an answer they can use again

A useful guide or service explanation can help customers after its publication date. Your sales and support teams can share it when the same questions arise.

Someone still needs to keep it accurate. Review changes to prices, processes and availability, and check whether customers or your team use the resource. Include that upkeep when assessing the cost of SEO.

### Take the next step

Once someone thinks you might be suitable, give them an appropriate way to enquire. That might be a call, a booking or a quote form asking for the details needed to assess the job.

Follow suitable requests through to their outcome. If few progress, examine response times, capacity and the offer itself alongside the work that brought people to the website.

## Will AI search mean fewer website visits?

Some people may get enough information from an AI answer without visiting a website. Research gives businesses a reason to take that possibility seriously.

Pew Research Center analysed browsing data from 900 US adults covering 68,879 unique Google searches in March 2025. People clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits with an AI summary, compared with 15% without one. A source link within the summary received a click in 1% of visits with a summary.

The study was observational, the queries differed, and researchers reconstructed search results in April after recording the browsing. It describes that US sample, rather than predicting Australian traffic or proving that AI caused the same loss for every website. [Pew's original research and method](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/).

Look at what makes your important pages worth visiting. Customers may need your service details, quote process or an example of your work before deciding. Track whether those pages contribute useful enquiries as well as visits.

## Google and ChatGPT work differently

Both can help someone discover information, but their controls and the way they present answers differ.

### What to check for Google

For a page to qualify as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, Google says it must be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet. There's no special AI schema requirement, and eligibility doesn't guarantee selection. [Google's AI-feature documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

Ask your web team to check access and indexing if important pages aren't being found. They should also check the Search generative AI control in Search Console. Google's help page describes inclusion as the default, with settings that can be inherited from a parent property. AI training preferences are separate from this inclusion control. [Search generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024).

Google also describes retrieving relevant pages and issuing related searches, called query fan-out, when building an AI answer. A result for the original search and a citation in an answer are different things to observe. This describes Google's approach, rather than every assistant's system. [Google's explanation of retrieval and query fan-out](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

### What to check for ChatGPT Search

OpenAI separates its search crawler, OAI-SearchBot, from GPTBot, which crawls content that may be used to train foundation models. Their controls are independent. A website can allow search access while disallowing GPTBot. Access alone doesn't guarantee a citation. [OpenAI's crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).

ChatGPT may search automatically or when requested. Responses that use search may include citations, but results and citations can be incomplete, outdated or incorrect. When reviewing an answer about your business, check the description and any linked sources. [OpenAI's ChatGPT search help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt).

## What should you fix first?

Choose work that addresses a problem you can identify. This table is a practical decision aid, rather than a rule from a search platform.

| What you're seeing | Where to start | What to check afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| Important service pages can't be accessed or indexed as intended | Have the technical problem diagnosed and repaired | Inspection results and the affected pages |
| Customers ask buying questions your website doesn't answer well | Improve a small set of service pages | The questions received and the suitability of enquiries |
| Suitable enquiries arrive but few progress | Review the offer and follow-up process | Response times, bookings and accepted quotes |
| You don't know how the business appears in AI search | Observe a small, defined set of relevant questions | Mentions, linked sources, accuracy and attributable visits |
| Budget or delivery capacity is tight | Compare the next SEO improvement with other priorities | Full costs, demand, margins and the work you can fulfil |

Agree on review points before starting. A team can schedule a repair or page update. Being found, receiving an enquiry and winning the work depend on additional factors. A fixed ranking promise gives you little basis for planning those outcomes.

## An example: finding an office cleaner in Brisbane

Imagine a commercial cleaning company serving a defined part of Brisbane. This is a hypothetical example, not a client result.

Someone might search for “commercial office cleaning Brisbane”. Another person might ask what to check before hiring a company for after-hours cleaning. The searches concern the same service, but they call for different information.

The company could explain its coverage and service scope, how a site assessment works and what goes into a quote. It could address access and scheduling for after-hours jobs. An original example of a cleaning scope could show what the work involves, provided it reflects the company's operation.

A quote form could ask for location, premises type, approximate size and preferred schedule. The team could then record whether the request fits the service and whether it leads to a site visit or accepted quote.

The change aims to help suitable prospects understand the service and send a useful request. Reviewing those requests and their outcomes would help the company decide what to improve next. No result in Google or ChatGPT is assumed in this example.

## A manageable way to get started

Pick one priority service and a realistic service area. Read the relevant pages as a new customer would, review available search data and ask your team which questions come up before a booking or quote.

Address the most consequential gap first. That may be an access problem, an unclear explanation or missing evidence. Record what you changed and when, so you have something concrete to review.

Apply the same factual standards if you use AI to help write. Google's spam policies address large-scale production intended to manipulate rankings without helping users, however the pages were created. [Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

To explore AI presence, use a small question set about your services, locations and customer decisions. Record the platform, date, mode, language and location context, then repeat observations under consistent conditions. Keep a brand mention separate from a recommendation or a linked source.

Aleyda Solis's prompt-library framework offers a professional method for choosing questions that reflect your audience. Your observations remain a sample, not an official ranking or a complete measure of Australian demand. [Solis's prompt-library framework](https://www.aleydasolis.com/en/ai-search/ai-search-prompt-library/).

## Follow enquiries through to outcomes

Being seen, receiving a visit and winning a customer are different stages. Keeping them separate helps you understand where the work is helping and where a problem remains.

| Stage | What you can record | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Website readiness | Access and indexing checks | Whether technical obstacles were found or resolved |
| Search or AI presence | Impressions, observed mentions and linked sources | Where the business appeared within the report or sample |
| Website visits | Attributable visits and useful actions | What people did on the site within the limits of tracking |
| Enquiries | Forms, connected calls and qualified requests | Whether suitable people made contact |
| Business outcomes | Bookings, accepted quotes, customers and contribution | What those opportunities were worth to the business |

As checked on 16 October 2026, Google's Generative AI performance report documents impressions for AI Overviews and AI Mode, with dimensions including pages, countries and devices. It may be absent when impressions are insufficient. The documented fields aren't a complete query, click or revenue report. [Search Console report help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139).

People may encounter your business without clicking, return directly or use several channels before contacting you. Record what you can connect to an enquiry and where information is missing. Consider seasonality, advertising, platform changes and competitors before attributing a change to a page edit.

## Two questions to ask an agency

### Why are you recommending llms.txt?

Google says Search ignores llms.txt, so the file shouldn't be sold as a Google visibility requirement. Another system may use it. Ask which system the recommendation concerns and what the work is meant to achieve. [Google's guidance on llms.txt](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

### What exactly are you promising to deliver?

Ask the agency to explain the work, platforms and reporting. If it promises a particular recommendation from an assistant, ask for the conditions and evidence behind that claim before relying on it.
