# What Is SEO? How Search Engine Optimisation Works for Your Business

**SEO stands for search engine optimisation.** It is the work of helping search engines find and understand your website, and helping people find useful pages through unpaid search results.

For your business, that might mean being found by someone comparing a service, researching a problem or looking for a provider. The aim is to bring the right person to a page that helps them decide what to do next.

That takes more than adding keywords. Your pages need to be accessible, explain what you offer and answer the questions behind a search. Here is what that work looks like.

## A Perth example: helping someone plan their garden

Say you run a landscaping business in Perth. Your website has good photographs, but one short page lists every service without explaining the projects you take on. This is a hypothetical example, not a Ranking Rebels client result.

Someone searching for “landscaping services Perth” needs to know whether you handle their kind of project and work in their area. A clearer service page, genuine examples of your work and an easy way to request an estimate give them something useful to act on.

## What does “organic search” mean?

Organic search results are unpaid listings selected by a search engine. You do not pay Google an advertising fee each time someone clicks your ordinary organic listing. SEO work still has costs, which can include research, writing, development, specialist help and maintenance.

A results page can contain advertisements, website listings and local business results. Work on your website and work on an eligible Google Business Profile can support the same business, but they involve different tasks.

There is also a difference between someone searching for your company name and someone searching for a service. The first person may already know you. The second may be discovering possible providers for the first time. People researching a question may be further from making a decision. Your pages should account for those different needs.

## How does SEO work?

[Google describes three stages in how Search works](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/how-search-works):

1. **Crawling:** automated programs discover pages and retrieve their content.
2. **Indexing:** Google analyses a page and may store information about it in its search index.
3. **Serving results:** when someone searches, Google's systems select and order relevant results.

Publishing a page does not guarantee that Google will index it. Being indexed does not guarantee a prominent position for a particular search, even when the page meets Google's requirements.

The distinction helps you work out what needs attention. If Google cannot access your landscaping service page, rewriting its sales copy will not fix the access problem. If the page is indexed but gives prospective customers little useful information, submitting it again will not fix that mismatch either.

SEO improves the parts you can control, including access, content and connections between pages. Search engines decide which results to show, while competing pages and the results themselves can change.

## What does SEO actually involve?

The work usually falls into a few connected areas. Which one deserves attention first depends on what is holding your website back.

### Technical foundations

Important pages need to be accessible to search engines and usable by people. Technical work can involve navigation, links, page responses, mobile presentation and the handling of duplicate or moved pages.

A sitemap lists URLs to help search engines discover them. As [Google's sitemap guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/overview) explains, it does not guarantee crawling or indexing. Ask what problem a proposed technical change will solve, rather than judging the work by the length of a checklist.

[Core Web Vitals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) measure loading performance, responsiveness and visual stability. Improving an awkward mobile page can make it easier to use. A good performance score alone does not establish how relevant that page is to a search.

### Relevant content and on-page information

A service page should explain what you offer, whom you serve and what someone needs to know before enquiring. A helpful article has a different job: answer its question accurately, with enough detail for the reader.

Keyword research helps identify the language and needs behind searches. “Landscaping services Perth” and “how to plan a low-maintenance garden” suggest different needs. Your service page might explain project types, coverage and the estimate process. An educational guide might help someone consider options and prepare a project brief. Repeating the same sales pitch on both pages misses that difference.

Titles, headings and internal links help explain the page's subject and guide readers. [Google's title guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) recommends clear, descriptive wording. Google may generate different title text in its results, so the title you write is an input rather than an exact display control.

Useful content needs more than search phrases. Relevant examples, accurate information and evidence for material claims give people a reason to read it. Copying a competitor's outline and adding more words does not establish that value. Each new page should have a purpose, including any page about a suburb you serve.

### Links and information beyond your website

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Google's [link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable) explains how links help it discover pages and understand relevance. Their context matters, alongside where they come from.

Useful work could include providing accurate information to a relevant industry organisation or publishing a resource that others choose to reference. Buying links to manipulate rankings can breach [Google's link spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#link-spam).

If someone reports on links they have built for you, ask where those links come from and why they are useful. A count alone tells you little about whether the work is worthwhile.

### Local visibility

If you serve a particular area, an eligible Google Business Profile may also help people find and assess your business. Keep its information accurate and explain your actual service area. [Google's local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) describes relevance, distance and prominence as the main considerations.

Your profile and website have related roles. Editing one does not replace useful information on the other, and a page about a place you do not genuinely serve does not make your offer more relevant. Our guide to [improving your visibility on Google Maps](https://rankingrebels.com/blog/local-seo-google-maps-visibility/) explains the profile work and how to assess local enquiries. A business serving customers nationally may need a different approach.

## What are the benefits of SEO for a business?

SEO can help people discover your business and arrive better informed. Its value depends on relevant demand, competition, the quality of your pages and what happens after the visit.

| Potential benefit | How it helps | What it depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Reach people researching a relevant need | A useful page gives them a way to discover your business | Relevant demand and a page that earns visibility |
| Attract visits that fit your offer | Clear service details help people assess whether you can help | Pages that reflect your actual services and audience |
| Support informed enquiries | Answers and examples reduce avoidable uncertainty | Accurate information and an enquiry process that works |
| Keep useful resources working over time | A strong service page or guide can remain useful beyond a campaign | Continued accuracy, accessibility and competitiveness |

For the Perth landscaper, fewer visits to a clear service page could be more useful than a large audience outside the service area. A garden-planning guide may help people well before they are ready to ask for an estimate. Assess each page against the job it is meant to do.

Those are potential benefits, not promised results. SEO also sits alongside referrals, advertising and other ways customers find you. Whether to invest more depends on the opportunity, the full cost of the work and the alternatives available to your business.

## SEO vs Google Ads: what is the difference?

SEO concerns unpaid visibility. Google Ads buys advertising opportunities under the campaign's rules and pricing model. Paying for advertising does not buy a higher organic position. Google states that [advertising does not affect a site's organic presence](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo).

| Question | SEO | Google Ads |
|---|---|---|
| What are you working towards? | Visibility in unpaid search results | Delivery and results from a paid campaign |
| What costs are involved? | Research, implementation, content and ongoing work | Advertising spend plus campaign, creative and landing-page work |
| What can you directly change? | Your pages and supporting work, while search engines select results | Campaign settings, subject to platform rules and delivery conditions |
| What determines business value? | Relevant visits and the outcomes they support | Relevant paid interactions and the outcomes they support |

Neither is automatically the better choice. You might use advertising to test an offer while improving important website pages. A short campaign may need a different plan from an ongoing service. A confusing offer or a broken form can waste visits from either source.

## How long does SEO take, and is it ongoing?

There is no universal deadline. Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says some changes can take effect in hours, while others can take several months. It generally recommends allowing a few weeks before assessing changes in Search results.

That describes how changes may be reflected in Search, not a deadline for enquiries, revenue or first-page positions. Your starting point, implementation, competition, demand and the type of change all affect what to expect.

Ask for milestones that separate completed work from observed results. Fixing a broken link, improving a service page and seeing a sustained change in suitable enquiries are different milestones.

Some work is a defined project. Other tasks return when services change, information becomes outdated or new issues appear. Your ongoing plan should follow those needs. Publishing a fixed number of articles each month does not, by itself, demonstrate progress.

## How do you know whether SEO is working?

Start with what you want to understand. Are relevant people finding the page? Can they use it? Are their enquiries a good fit?

[Google Search Console's Performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553) shows visibility and clicks in Google Search. Analytics and your enquiry or sales records help explain what happens after a visit.

| What to review | Useful question | What still needs checking |
|---|---|---|
| Search visibility | Which searches and pages receive impressions and clicks? | Whether those searches relate to suitable demand |
| Click-through rate | How do clicks compare with impressions for relevant searches? | The query and the results shown affect that rate |
| Website activity | Do visitors reach useful pages and complete intended actions? | Tracking must be configured and checked |
| Enquiry quality | Are enquiries genuine and within your service scope? | A form event does not establish that an enquiry is suitable |
| Commercial outcomes | Which enquiries become customers, and what did the work cost? | Attribution does not establish that SEO alone caused a sale |

In GA4, you can designate important measured actions as [key events](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/13965727). Choose actions that match your goals and check that they record what you intend. A contact-button click and a successfully received enquiry are different things.

For the landscaping business, that means checking the services and locations people asked about, whether the team responded and which enquiries became projects. More enquiries will be difficult to turn into work if the team has no capacity to respond or deliver it.

Compare relevant pages and searches over meaningful periods, allowing for seasonality, brand activity and other changes. [Search Console clicks and analytics sessions measure different things](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/google-analytics-search-console), so do not expect the totals to match exactly.

Include writing, development, specialist and maintenance costs when assessing the investment. State the attribution method and its limitations. An average ranking or total traffic figure cannot tell the whole story, and a dashboard alone does not prove the extra revenue caused by each change.

## Does AI search change the fundamentals?

It changes parts of the search experience. For Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, [Google says existing SEO practices still apply](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). It does not require special AI markup or a new machine-readable file for inclusion.

That guidance is specific to Google's features and does not guarantee a citation. Clear, accessible and well-supported pages remain a useful foundation. ## Where should your business start?

Begin with the services you want to grow and the people you can help.

1. **Define your audience and offer.** Be clear about whom you serve, where you operate and which services matter most.
2. **Review the important pages.** Can people find them, understand the offer and take the next step?
3. **Understand different search needs.** Separate service searches, comparisons and educational questions instead of giving every phrase the same page.
4. **Record a starting point and improve a priority.** Choose a change you can explain, then compare appropriate measurements afterwards.
5. **Use the evidence to decide what comes next.** Keep, adjust or stop work according to useful outcomes and costs.

You can learn and do some of this yourself. Specialist help makes sense when the diagnosis, implementation or ongoing decisions exceed your team's time or capability. Ask a provider what they plan to change, why it should help and how they will assess progress. Guaranteed positions and activity reports without a clear purpose deserve scrutiny.
